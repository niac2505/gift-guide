The Quintessential Host

The Best Cheese in America

Not to brag, but I used to have an in with the best cheese in America. My best friend worked at Jasper Hill for many years. In that time, I became intimately acquainted with its funky, gooey, nutty, ashy products. Some of them, such as the cheddar and the blue, are the highest possible expression of cheeses you know and love. Others—the squashy Willoughby and molten Harbison—taste like nothing you’ve ever had before. If someone in your life throws dinner parties that seem unimprovable, give them a few months of Jasper Hill.

$105/month on Jasper Hill Farm

Marionette Replicas

Every time I have friends over and half-heartedly throw on some YouTube music videos, I think: If only I had $23,210 to buy the requisite set of replica marionettes, I would be entertaining these guys right now with a shot-perfect re-creation of “The Lonely Goatherd” from the 1965 film The Sound of Music. Granted, that’s a lot of money, and getting the goatherd’s yodeling to look just right will take significant practice, but it will be so worth it when your guests say, “What?” and also, “Why?”

$23,210 on Marionettes.cz

The Perfect Juicer

My household is such a big consumer of sour-sweet concoctions that we joke that citrus is the fifth element. Like fire, air, earth, and water, lemons resist taming: squirts in the eye, acid under the nails, seeds in the salad. But the Fluicer, a juice press that smashes with horizontal force, avoids mess and wastes no drop. Pour up your tangy treat of choice and celebrate man’s latest victory over nature.

$19.99 on Dreamfarm

Top-Tier Coffee

I am more pretentious and particular about my coffee than anything else. Imagine having coffee beans from all the best hipster roasters around the country brought to your doorstep each week. That’s what Trade offers. You fill out a questionnaire on how you take your coffee, what flavor profiles you prefer, and how adventurous you want to be. The company matches you with different shops that send you freshly roasted beans on the weekly. I’ve subscribed for years, and have expanded my palate every which way.

$66 for a three-month subscription on Trade

The Muscle Car of Espresso Machines

Espresso lovers have plenty of cheap, one-touch, self-timing options for the home kitchen. But jeweled Nespresso pods and Breville automation have their limits when caffeine addiction succumbs to the obsessive quest for the perfect pour. The Profitec Go, a German-engineered, Italian-made machine, operates more like a classic stick-shift muscle car than a shiny new Tesla. Every part is adjustable—the pressure, the temperature, the timing—which makes each shot an aspirational leap for the unreachable ideal. The hardware heats up slower than the competition. But in a world of creeping mediocrity and oppressive efficiency, we all must choose where to make our stand.

$1,199 on Whole Latte Love

Handcrafted Knives

Few tools are more essential to cooking than a good, sharp knife. Everett Noel, a cutler wunderkind, began making knives at 13, and taught himself how by watching YouTube videos. Fifteen years later, he has honed his craft into something sublime, and produces some seriously beautiful knives, including this paring knife. Based in the Sierra Nevada foothills, Everett makes each handle from locally sourced hardwood, much of it salvaged from fallen trees, and forges the blades from steel. The result is a functional work of art.

$100–$725 on Everett Noel Knives

The Silverware You Can Keep Gifting

This set will help you stop agonizing over what to get your kitchen-savvy friend for every gift-giving occasion. Sabre is known for its unique, stylish cutlery that comes in a bunch of colors and patterns. Pick one, and then give your friend something from the collection again and again. Bottle openers! Butter knives! Tart servers! More spoons! Soon, your friend will have a drawer full of matching tortoiseshell tableware, thanks to you.

$122.50 on Sabre Paris

The Joy of Cooking

Much like the Velveteen Rabbit knew he was loved because of his threadbareness, to know a cookbook is adored is to see it soiled with stains. My copy of The Joy of Cooking is splotched all over. When I was 19, my mother gave me this 1,000-page doorstopper, and since then, it’s taught me how to boil an egg, roll out fresh pasta, make Swedish meatballs, and perfect a bone broth. When people ask me, “Can I have your key-lime pie recipe?” I sheepishly admit that it’s not mine at all, but rather filched from the cookbook whose only misery is to be covered in food.

$45 on Bookshop

A Fun Cake Server

A cake or pie server is one of those hyper-specific things—no one needs one, but without it, whenever dessert time rolls around, you are left either using a boring spatula or, worse, wrangling a piece of cake awkwardly using two forks, which usually ends with losing half the slice on the table. Nothing screams “fun host” more than a hand-shaped server!
